The rain began just as the final whistle blew.

Arjun stood alone on the empty football field, mud clinging to his socks, the echo of the crowd still ringing in his ears. They had lost the district semifinal by one goal. One stupid, careless goal.

He replayed the moment again. Sixty-third minute. The score tied. He had passed the ball to Kabir—his best friend since class six, his striker, the one who never missed from that distance. The goal was open. It should have been simple.

But Kabir didn’t shoot.

Instead, he hesitated. Just long enough for the defender to steal the ball and launch a counterattack. Thirty seconds later, it was in their net.

At first, Arjun thought it was pressure. Nerves. It happened to everyone.

Until he heard the whispers.

“They paid him.”

“His cousin plays for the other team.”

“Didn’t you see? He didn’t even try.”

Arjun had refused to believe it. Not Kabir. Not the guy who shared lunch with him every day, who stayed late after practice, who knew his fears about never being good enough.

But now, as he watched from behind the equipment shed, he saw Kabir talking to the rival team’s captain. Not arguing. Not upset.

Laughing.

And then—the handshake. A folded envelope passed quietly from one palm to another.

The world didn’t shatter. It didn’t explode. It simply tilted.

Kabir turned and saw him standing there.

For a moment, neither of them spoke. The rain grew heavier.

“I was going to tell you,” Kabir said weakly.

Arjun felt something colder than anger settle in his chest. Not rage. Not shouting.

Just absence.

“You already did,” he replied.

And as he walked away, he realized something worse than losing a match.

He had lost a friend.

And some losses don’t come with a rematch.
